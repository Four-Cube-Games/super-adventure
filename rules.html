Getting started

Super Adventure is a Discord Activity: the game opens inside Discord, on a computer, in a browser or on a phone, and you walk its world tile by tile. Type /play or /activity in a channel of a server that has the bot. Servers are approved by hand, so a new one waits until it's let in (see Play).

Your partner

The first time you open it, you choose your partner from three: creatures that can evolve, each of a different type, and one of them always one of the region's original starters. Everyone in a server is offered the same three. You also choose how your trainer looks, and can change it any time.

You set out with 5 balls and 500 coins.

Controls

  • On a keyboard: walk with the arrow keys or WASD, and hold Shift to run. A (talk, confirm, next line) is Enter, Space or Z; B (back, and the menu) is Esc, Backspace or X. Options › Controls lists them in the game.
  • On a phone: walk with the on-screen pad. Options can hide the pad; with it hidden, tap a square to walk there. Hold B while you move to run; on its own B opens the menu.
  • Running is twice the pace and only quicker. It doesn't work while surfing.
  • A, or a tap, speaks to whoever you're facing. Tapping another player opens their trainer card.
  • Held upright, the screen sits above the pad and A/B, like a handheld. Turned sideways, the pad goes left of the screen and A/B right.
  • Small buttons at the bottom middle open ☰ Start and show or hide the pad (✥ Select). With the pad showing, tapping the map doesn't walk you.
  • The game is silent for now, so your own music or podcast keeps playing. Text speed and animations are in Options.

Moving about

Every place is laid out from your world's seed, so the same place is always the same, and no two worlds are alike. The world is a line of areas: routes, caves, tunnels and bike roads, broken up by towns. In older worlds the way on is at the top of an area and the way back at the bottom; in newer ones a route's way on may lie on any side (see The island).

The island

A world made now is an island grown from its seed, with coasts, mountain ranges, rivers that run to the sea and one volcano. Your towns and routes are laid across it in the same order as ever, so a route can come in at the west and leave at the north, heading whichever way the land takes it. The land decides what each stretch is: its biome, caves and tunnels or open mountain trails where the road climbs over high ground, gym towns in their type's home biome where they can be, and the Elite Four up high. Rivers are crossed by bridges, and there is a town on the beach and one on a small island you reach by Surf, across sea routes with islets, swimmers and sailors. The last stretch before the Elite Four is always a cave. Every place can be reached with the badges you hold by then. The Map in the start menu draws the island, with fog over the stretches your server hasn't opened yet and a cursor where you are. Tap any place on it, or step to one with the pad, and a bubble names it and says what it is: a gym and its type, a route, a cave, a hideout, a shrine, or where you are; under the fog it says only that the place isn't explored yet. A gym you've beaten wears a gold badge at its corner, and a hideout you've cleared or a shrine whose legend you've woken a gold tick. Worlds made before this keep their old layout and board.

Some caves on the island change as you cross them: open grass first, then a cave or tunnel through a cave mouth, then a mountain trail, or grass again where the road comes back down. Each open part has its own wild creatures and a trainer of its own, a local on the grass, a hiker, explorer or camper on the trail. About one cave in nine does this. Victory Road, caves before the first gym and caves you enter from the north stay whole.

New ground

The roads are open: walk as far as you like in a day. Two things stop you, the trainers on a route, which you beat to move on, and ground your party can't cross yet, such as water, a waterfall or a rock face, which badges open. The big places open one a day (see Days and seasons). A hideout's or shrine's entrance is a gate beside the road, in the area before it, and leaving puts you back at the gate. Walk into one before its day and you're told when it opens: "The hideout's door is barred. It opens in 2 days." A gym's door says "The gym opens tomorrow."

Walking back

Walk off the bottom of an area into the one behind, and forward again over ground you've cleared. It's free, costs no day, and works in every world. Leaving a gym or gauntlet part way puts its trainers back, as losing there would, but costs no coins.

Trainers spot you

Each trainer faces one way and sees three or four squares straight ahead, stopped by walls, other people and any change of level. Walk into their sight and they call out, walk up and fight. You can't slip past, and you can run from wild creatures but never from a trainer. Trainers suit where they stand: Youngsters and Lasses on routes, Hikers underground, Bikers on the bike road, Ace Trainers in gyms, Grunts in hideouts. Each has a line to start the fight and one to end it.

The lie of the land

  • Every stretch between towns has a biome: meadow, forest, coast, mountain, snow, desert, volcano or swamp. The way to the first gym is all meadow; after that the land shifts gradually, a gym's type pulls its stretch toward home (a water gym by the coast, a fire gym near a volcano), and the last stretch, to the Elite Four, is mountain.
  • Routes have height and water: terraces joined by stairs, rivers with bridges, lakes, and the sea along the coast. You climb only by stairs: north or south through a flight set in a cliff face, or east or west down a flight on a side bank, never up a cliff itself. A hill's lowest drop may stand two squares tall.
  • Caves have one to three floors, deeper ones later on, and the last cave before the Elite Four has three. Hideouts have floors too. Walk off the top edge to go up a floor and off the bottom to come back down; each floor has its own trainers and item balls.
  • Caves in the second half of the map are dark, showing only a circle around you, until someone in your party can use Flash.
  • Bike roads and tunnels are packed with trainers, with no way around.

Field moves

Badges open new ways across the land. They take no move slot, and no particular creature has to know them: walk into the obstacle and whoever in your party suits it best does it, and the game says who. If nobody fits, any healthy creature does it. Obstacles are back the next time you come in.

Nothing wild bothers you while you surf. On newer worlds a signpost on the grass a square or two before deep or falling water tells you which move would get you across, and how many badges it needs.

Item balls

Balls lying on the ground hold an item. Look behind small trees and, from the fourth gym, cracked rocks and boulders, across streams once you can surf, and loose about routes and caves. You find each once, what's inside is the same for everyone in a world, and it gets better further along.

Other players

Anyone else in the same place walks about live, with their name over their head. They don't block your way.

Towns and cities

Every town has a Center and a Mart, each a room you walk into; every Center wears a medical cross and every Mart a coin sign over its door. About two houses in five open: knock and walk in to a cottage or a house with a kitchen and a bedroom, furnished to suit the town and home to a resident or two, and now and then one gives you a small gift, once. The other doors stay shut. Towns grow their own streets, with squares, yards and gardens, laid out by where they are, and each town's locals have something to say that changes daily, often a real tip about what's ahead. A few tame creatures live in towns: they answer with a cry and never fight.

Every town has a character

Tower and dojo trainers don't ask for rematches.

Rivals, gifts, dojo halls and skirmishes

Every stretch ends at something to aim for: a gym, a hideout, a shrine, the preserve, or, in a town with none of those, a rival, a gift, a dojo hall or a skirmish. Each is yours once and stays done.

  • The rival started when you did, and waits just inside the town's way in with a name like Stinky or Poo Poo Head, the same name wherever you meet. They lead with the partner that beats yours, backed by two or three more of the region's creatures, never a legend. They fight you once in each such town, up to three times in a world, a little above the area's level and stronger every time.
  • The gift comes from a resident in one of the town's houses, who gives each player one creature: a kind the routes behind you haven't shown, never a legend, at the area's level. Before the second gym it is one of the gentlest. It joins your party, or goes to the box if the party is full.
  • The dojo hall turns up from the second gym on. Two or three students of one type wait in a row, then their master, and you fight them in order, as at a gym but with no badge. Beat a student and they stay beaten, even if the master wins. When the master falls, they let you pick one of two creatures of that type, there and then, once. If you walked off without choosing, talk to the master again. No two halls in a world share a type, and the early ones go easy on you.
  • The skirmish only comes before the third gym: two villain grunts pick a fight in town, one after the other, at the area's level. Beat both and they leave you a ball, potion, revive or cure fit for the town, once, and let slip where the next hideout lies.

On newer worlds the first gym ends the first stretch, and the first day opens it.

Cities

A town with a gym is a city of two to four parts. You arrive in the town part, with the Center, Mart, houses and the preserve's gate if it has one. Walk on through a far edge to the next part. Through the fourth gym a city is two parts; the fifth and sixth add a market quarter, and the seventh and eighth a park with a canal too. The last part is always the gym district, and the way on to the next stretch leaves from there.

Wild creatures

Tall grass and the floors of caves and tunnels wake creatures as you walk, and every route, cave, tunnel and bike road has two to five more (one or two on the way to the first gym, two or three on the way to the second) wandering in view: grass creatures on the grass, water creatures in the water, cave creatures on the floor. Walk into one and the battle starts. Once you've met it, it's gone for you for the rest of the day, however the battle ended, and the next day brings new ones.

A rare one turns up every two hours from 7 am to 9 pm, your server's time: an uncommon or rare creature, never a legend or one of the season's starters, somewhere on ground the server has opened. Everyone sees the same one, with a sparkle over it, and the map says where. Knock it out or run and it's still there for anyone; the first to catch it keeps it. Your server hears when one appears and who caught it.

  • Where you are changes what you meet. Caves have fewer trainers and more unusual creatures. A biome tilts its routes toward its own kinds: water and flying on the coast; ground, rock and fire in the desert; ice and water in the snow; fire and rock by a volcano; grass and bug in the forest; poison, water and grass in the swamp; rock, fighting, flying and dragon in the mountains.
  • The first routes hold the weakest creatures: before the first gym, mostly small normal, flying, bug, grass and poison kinds, and before the second gym nothing too strong. Rares stay hidden until after the second gym.
  • Rares hide in one spot each, usually a cave, and some aren't in your world at all. Which ones you can't get changes from world to world.
  • Shinies come in their own colours, with a sparkle and a ✨ by the name. They're no stronger, they stay shiny when caught and evolved, and your server hears when you catch one.
  • Beating a wild creature pays 5 coins a level.

Battles

Battles are animated: creatures slide in, health bars drain, and the text waits for a tap. They play like the classics, with the later games' best pieces added.

Turns and moves

  • Each turn you pick from Fight, Bag, Run and Party: use a move, use an item or throw a ball, run from a wild battle, or switch.
  • Switching calls yours back and sends another out. It costs your turn, like an item, and the other side gets a free hit on whoever comes in.
  • The faster creature moves first. Priority moves jump the queue whatever the speed (Quick Attack, Extreme Speed, Aqua Jet), and a few, like Roar and Counter, always go last. Prankster puts its user's status moves ahead.
  • A move of the creature's own type hits half as hard again, and every hit varies a little, from 85% to 100%.
  • Each creature knows up to four moves. A move it learns with four full is skipped, not lost: the Move Tutor in any Center teaches it back (see Centers).

Moves that do more

  • Healing: Recover and its like heal half your HP. Rest sleeps two turns, cures whatever ailed it and wakes at full HP; holding a berry that cures sleep wakes it at once.
  • Draining moves heal half of what they deal. Take Down and Double-Edge cost recoil.
  • Leech Seed saps 1/8 of the target's HP a turn for whoever is out on your side. Grass types can't be seeded.
  • Hitting two to five times: two hits 35% of the time, three 35%, four 15%, five 15%. Each hit can be critical.
  • Wrap, Bind and Fire Spin trap the target for 4 or 5 turns at 1/8 a turn. A trapped creature can't run or switch, but can still fight.
  • Hyper Beam costs the next turn to recharge. Dream Eater only works on a sleeping target. Stockpile stores up to three, toughening you as it does; Spit Up and Swallow spend them all.

Abilities

Every creature has one of its species' abilities, and keeps it when it evolves. One wild creature in fifty has its species' hidden ability instead, and your party screen marks it. Levitate dodges ground moves, Intimidate lowers the other's Attack as it comes out, Swift Swim doubles speed in rain, Static may paralyze whoever touches it. Abilities that care what kind of move it is work as in the games: Static only on moves that make contact, Iron Fist powers up punches, Soundproof blocks sounds. When one does something, the battle says so, and the party screen says what each one does. About two in three do something here; the rest are for things this game doesn't have, like double battles. Opponents know your ability too.

Held items

Each creature can hold one item, given from the bag and taken back whenever you're out of battle. Catch a wild creature that's holding something and it comes too.

  • Berries are sold in Marts and eaten when they're needed: at half HP, or the moment an ailment strikes.
  • Gear is earned, never bought or sold. Every gym leader hands over the booster for their type, which makes those moves hit 20% harder. Every hideout boss leaves Leftovers, a Quick Claw or a Focus Band, one you haven't got while there's one left. The mountain dojo's master gives one too.
  • An Everstone, held, stops evolution by level (see Growing).

Gear, like the Exp. Share, belongs to that world's run: nothing you carry between worlds makes you better at the game.

Winning and losing

  • A trainer pays 25 coins a level of their strongest creature, so an early trainer pays about a ball and a gym leader far more.
  • If your whole party faints, you black out: you wake in the last Center you passed, in front of the nurse, with 5% of your coins gone. Your day goes on, and for an hour after that, blacking out again costs you nothing.
  • If both sides' last creatures fall in the same action, the attacker's side wins when its own faint came from its attack: recoil, say, or the other side's ability. Poison, burns, weather and the like strike the faster creature first at the end of a turn, so if both fall then, the faster side loses, and a coin decides between equals. A win that leaves your whole party fainted still carries you to the last Center, healed, with your coins kept and the trainers you had beaten in a gym or hideout still beaten.

Growing and evolving

Experience

Every creature that was out against a foe and is still standing splits its experience evenly, and catching one gives the same experience as knocking it out. The third gym leader hands you the Exp. Share, an item a creature holds. While one of yours holds it, each knockout's experience is split: half among those who fought, half to the holder, even if it sat out. Give it to whoever should catch up, or take it off to keep everything for the fighters.

Natures, potential and training

No two creatures are quite alike. Every wild creature, and your partner, has a nature: most raise one stat by a tenth and lower another, a few do neither. It has potential, 0 to 31 in each stat, graded S to D on your party screen. Both are fixed for life. Beating a creature trains everyone who earns experience from it in the stats its species teaches, up to 252 in a stat and 510 in all.

Trainers' creaturesPotential in every stat
Most trainers8
Ace Trainers, grunts and shrine seekers16
Gym leaders, hideout bosses and dojo masters24
The Elite Four and champions31

Evolution

  • By level: once a creature reaches its level, it evolves after a battle you win, one step at a time, and learns anything its new form learns at that level. A fainted creature waits for the next win. You watch it happen, and pressing B stops it; a creature you stopped asks again once it has grown another level.
  • By stone: use it from the bag. For some creatures, the stone you choose decides what they become.
  • By trade: a creature that evolved by trading evolves when it's traded to you, or with a Linking Cord from the bag.
  • The Everstone, held, stops evolution by level and is never used up. Take it back and it evolves after its next win. A stone or the Cord still works on it.
  • Every hideout's boss leaves an evolution item: one that evolves something you own, if anything does.

One town in the middle of the map, between the fourth and sixth gyms, keeps a fenced preserve behind it. Pay the warden at the gatehouse and walk in.

Centers and Marts

The Center

  • The nurse heals your whole party for nothing, as often as you like, curing every ailment. It never ends your day.
  • The PC holds your box.
  • The Move Tutor teaches a creature again any move it has learned by its level, for free, in place of one it knows. The new move keeps the share of PP the old one had left, so swapping isn't a free refill.
  • A board shows today's goal, and how many in your server have met it.

Inside a gym's town, healing at the Center while you're part way through the gym puts its trainers back (see Gyms).

The Mart

Every town has a Mart. The first two towns' Marts never close; after that, a Mart is open about two days in three, and what's on the shelf changes. When it's open it always has plain balls and Potions, and better stock further into the map. Your whole server sees the same shelf on the same day, and nobody can buy anyone else out. The counter has Buy and Sell: while the Mart is open, anything but gear sells back for half its price. Using an item in battle takes your turn; outside battle, it's free. Some things are never on a shelf (Ethers, Elixirs and Max Revives, found lying about instead), some are sold only in a market town's department store, with other Marts selling them now and then from the 3rd gym's town on (the drinks), and herbs are sold only in farming and mushroom towns and the department store.

Repels skip a wild encounter whose creature is below your first able creature's level, and a smaller Repel never shortens one already running. They don't stop creatures wandering in view, trainers, roaming legends, the preserve or the tower. The steps left show beside the menu, and when they run out you can use another. The Escape Rope doesn't work in towns, other buildings, the Elite Four's halls or the preserve. Battle items can't be used from the bag outside battle; they use your turn (not Fluffy Tail), and aren't allowed in battles against other players.

Buy 10 or more balls at once, of any kind, and a Premier Ball, a plain ball, comes free. It is never sold.

TMs teach a creature a move, and you keep them for good: teaching one never uses it up, so any number of your creatures can learn from it, and you can't buy a second copy. A department store has a shelf of 8 TMs, and any other Mart from the 3rd gym's town on has a shelf of 3. A town's shelf is always the same ones, not a new draw each day. A TM costs 1,000 to 8,000 by the move (2,000 for a move that only changes things), and stronger moves are sold later in the map. Use one from the Bag, outside battle, on a creature that can learn it: some can't, and one that already knows the move refuses it. If the creature knows four moves, it asks which to forget. A move taught this way counts as one it has known, so the Move Tutor can teach it again. TMs sell back at half price, and none are found lying about.

Found, never sold: Ether and Max Ether restore 10 or all the PP of one move you pick, Elixir and Max Elixir do it for every move, and a Max Revive brings a creature back at full HP. Ethers and Elixirs work on a fainted creature too.

What an item is called and what it costs can differ a little from region to region.

Gyms and gauntlets

Eight gyms, each a random type, drawn from the region and as different from each other as they can be. Levels climb steadily: the first leader is level 14 and the last level 56. The way to the first gym starts gently, with at least three stretches of wild starting at level 2 and climbing only a couple of levels a stretch, so the first routes top out around level 7. The stretches before the second gym stay easy too.

Trainers before the leader

Every gym has trainers before its leader: two at the first gym, rising to four at the eighth, using the gym's type a few levels below the leader. You fight them in order, back to back, and the leader won't see you until they're all down. Lose to any of them, or step out to heal at the Center, and they're all waiting again, until only the leader is left (see checkpoints).

Every gym is a puzzle

Between the door and the leader, each gym is built around a puzzle chosen by the leader's type, made from the seed and always solvable, and never one you can just walk round: the way through takes the ice, switches, panels or arrows again and again. Later gyms have bigger ones. Once you hold a gym's badge, it comes back solved when you run it again.

The attendant

About halfway through every gym, hideout, shrine and the Elite Four stands an attendant. Speak to them and your whole party is healed, for free, as often as you like. Nothing else changes: the trainers you've beaten stay beaten.

Checkpoints

Once only the last trainer of a run is left, you're at its checkpoint. From then on, losing, walking out or healing at the Center keeps everyone else beaten, so the next try is against the one who beat you.

Leaders fight smart

Gym leaders, everyone in a hideout or at a shrine, the Elite Four and champions read your creature's type. If it hits theirs hard, they call it back for one that takes it better, and when one falls they send out whichever suits the fight. Their ace always comes out last. Route and gym trainers send theirs out in order.

What a leader gives

Their badge, the booster for their type, and balls the first time you beat them. Leaders talk about their type and hand the badge over themselves.

Reruns and rematches

  • Reruns. Once you've won a badge or cleared a hideout, speak to the beaten leader or boss to run it again, as often as you like. Its trainers rise to where you've got to, the last at the level of the next gym you haven't beaten, and they switch to suit the fight. A rerun pays experience and training, but no coins, worthiness, badge or gear, and isn't on your record. Walking out or healing ends it.
  • Trainer rematches. Each day, three trainers you've beaten on routes, in caves, tunnels and on bike roads want a rematch, picked for you alone. They stand with a red VS over their head. Speak to them and they'll ask first, telling you how strong they've grown; say no and they'll wait. Their strongest creature comes back halfway from where it was toward a little below the next gym's level, and never more than two levels under your own strongest. They pay and count like any trainer.

Hideouts

From the third gym on, a couple of villain hideouts sit in the way. Each has three or four grunts, then a boss, fought straight through. A floor runs from the bottom up: an entrance hall, a doorway guarded by a grunt, a room, a second wall and room, the puzzle, and the boss's room at the top with the stairs on. Each kind of hideout (foundry, market, lab, cellar and so on) looks its own way.

  • Bigger hideouts have up to three floors, the boss on the top one; in the first half of the map, never more than two.
  • Every floor has a puzzle between its grunts and the way up: switches and barriers, warp panels, or arrows. Once you've walked past one it stays solved for good, and coming down onto a floor from above counts as passing it.
  • An attendant heals your team for free about halfway in.
  • Black out and you start again from the first grunt, unless it was the boss who beat you: then the grunts stay beaten.
  • The boss leaves an evolution item and a piece of gear. Your first hideout cleared earns a bandana for your wardrobe, and each one cleared is worth 3 worthiness.

Shrines, legends and blessings

Some of the region's legends sleep at shrines in the back half of the map. The rest roam: each keeps to one route of the late wilderness a day and moves on overnight, turning up about once in a hundred searches there, well above the level of anything around it. A world can also borrow up to three legends from other regions installed on the bot; visitors only ever roam. An oasis town's seer names the next shrine and where each roamer is today.

Shrines

Two to four seekers guard a shrine, digging for what's buried there, with an attendant on the way up. Beat them all, and the legend sleeps on its altar: speak to it to wake it. A shrine's legend is six levels above that stretch's gym leader.

The aura

A legend met in the wild, at a shrine or roaming, fights with half as much again in every stat but HP, and the battle says so. Caught, it loses the aura and is only as strong as its level.

When a legend gets away

Catch it or beat it and it's settled for good. If it runs, or beats you, it watches you, and faces you again once you've proved yourself. Your chances are yours alone: losing yours costs nobody else theirs. Roamers just keep turning up, rarely.

Beat a legend instead of catching it, and the creature that landed the knockout is permanently 10% stronger in every stat, and you earn a scarf for your wardrobe. It's the one power-up training can't give, so passing on the catch is a real choice.

The Elite Four and Hall of Fame

Beat all eight gyms, then the Elite Four and the Champion, back to back, and you've won the map. The four are the same in every region: Ice, Fighting, Ghost and Dragon, and a Normal Champion. Their teams change from world to world but always come from that region, keep to their type and are fully evolved and strong, never legends. The Elite Four is a castle you climb in tiers: in through the gatehouse to a forecourt, then a court for each member, dressed for their type, with a stair up to the next, and the Champion's keep at the top. A door on opens once its keeper is beaten. An attendant stands halfway, and lose to the Champion and the four stay beaten.

The Hall of Fame

Your winning team enters the Hall of Fame exactly as it stood: every creature, level and move. Your server hears about it, a cup goes on the back of your trainer card, you earn a cape, and your team can turn up in other players' rematches.

After you win

Once a day you can run the Elite Four again, and real champions take the seats: other players' Hall of Fame teams, from any region, with their exact creatures, levels and moves. Champions take the last seats, and a seat nobody fills keeps its original member. Each seat pays like any trainer, and there are new faces every day. Win a rematch and you earn a crown. You'll find them in the Elite Four's chambers, each champion wearing their own look. Speak to the first seat to take on today's Elite Four, then walk on to each chamber in turn; a seat fights as soon as you speak to them, and one ahead of the next sends you back to wait. When the run's done, the seats say new faces take them tomorrow.

Days and seasons

This is the biggest difference from the classics. A server's world is shared, and one big place opens for everyone each day.

One big place a day

  • The big places open one a day, in map order: gyms, villain hideouts and legend shrines, with the Elite Four last. On the season's day n, the first n are open, and once open they stay open. The first day opens the first gym. In a short season more open each day, so the Elite Four opens two days before the end.
  • Any open gym can be taken in any order, though gyms get tougher along the map. The Elite Four needs all eight badges as well as its day. Hideouts and shrines are side trips, for their rewards.
  • Nobody waits for anyone else. You can walk as far as you like, and you can join on any day of a season.
  • Healing never costs a day.
  • Private worlds, and worlds with no season, have everything open.

When a day starts

At midnight where your server is. Admins pick the time zone with /timezone set; until they do, days turn over at midnight UTC. Changing it never costs or gives anyone a day: the day in progress runs on to the next midnight in the new zone, so it can get longer but never shorter. Private worlds stay on UTC. When a new day opens something, the bot posts what opened in the server's announcement channel. The season's first day isn't posted, since the new season is, and after a gap one post covers everything opened since.

The daily goal

Each day has one goal for everyone in a world: catch 2 to 4 creatures, beat 2 to 4 trainers, or knock out 4 to 8 wild ones. Meeting it pays 300 coins, plus 100 for each badge, once a day. It shows at the top of the start menu and on the board in every Center, with how many in your server have met it. Challenges, live battles and raids don't count toward beating trainers.

Seasons, and your server's pace

A server's world lasts a season. When the season ends, the world is generated anew from a new seed: new towns, routes, gyms and shrines, in the region your server voted for, and everyone sets off again. Admins choose its length with /pace set:

PaceSeason
Relaxed45 days

Brisk21 days
Sprint14 days

Standard is where every server starts. A new pace changes the season's length from the next season, so nobody's season is cut short. A private world has no seasons. When you choose your partner, it tells you how many days are left.

Regions, and the vote

There are nine regions, and a world is one of them, with that region's creatures and the moves they learned there. In the last three days of a season, everyone playing in your server votes on where the next one goes with /region vote, and can change their vote until it closes; /region show has the tally. The most votes wins, a tie is drawn between the leaders, and with no votes at all it's the bot's home region.

Raids

A week into each season, and every week after, a raid boss turns up in your server's world for two days (one, if it lands on the season's last). It's something strong and fully evolved from the region, a little above the gym the season should have reached by now, and never a legend, with HP enough for everyone playing.

  • /raid shows it and how it stands; its Fight button opens the Activity, and the fight's last lines say how much you took off.
  • You can fight it three times in all, across both days, with a fit copy of your team, so it costs you nothing.
  • Whatever you knock off counts for everyone.
  • When it falls, everyone who helped gets one throw at catching one: likely, but not certain. Caught, it joins you at the boss's level.

Trades

Trading happens face to face, in the game. Walk up to another player in your server's world, face them and press A (or tap them): a menu offers Battle, Trade and Card. There's no list of players, so to trade you have to find each other. Private worlds have nobody to meet.

They're asked first: "Ann wants to trade!" with Yes and No, and No already chosen. No answer in 30 seconds is a no, and so is either of you walking away. While you're linked, you both stand still and a LINK tag shows over you.

  1. You both see both parties and boxes, side by side.
  2. Each of you picks one creature to give, and sees the other's pick with its level, nature, moves and held item.
  3. Changing a pick, on either side, clears both confirmations. When you've both pressed Trade, the creatures swap.

You can't trade away the last creature able to fight, Held items go with the creature. A traded creature arrives healed, and you can trade it on, or straight back, at once. A creature that evolves by trading evolves as it arrives, unless it's holding an Everstone. A trade left alone for five minutes is called off, and your server hears about finished trades.

{% include feedback.html topic="Trades" %}

Battling other players

Walk up to a player as for a trade, press A and choose Battle. They're asked first, the same way, and you both stand still while linked.

The battle is live, with both of you in the game at once, each seeing it from your own side. You each pick a move or a switch, and the turn plays out on both screens when you've both picked. You have two minutes a turn, counted down on a clock: whoever hasn't picked by then loses, and if neither has, it's called off. Give up concedes, and when one of yours faints, you choose who comes out next. If you close the game or lose your connection for more than a minute you lose, but reloading inside the minute puts you back where you were.

You each fight with a fit copy of your team, so there's no need to heal first. No items, running isn't an option, and there's no experience, money or blackout in it. The result goes on both your win-loss records, with a tally between you two: "You lead Ann 3 to 2."

Looks and the wardrobe

Your trainer walks every map, steps into every battle from behind before sending the first creature out, and stands on your trainer card, dressed how you choose. Wardrobe in the start menu changes it, and shows you front and back.

  • Free to change, any time: your skin, from ten tones lightest to darkest; your hair, any colour at all, picked on a colour slider with five shades from near black to silver, so purple, teal and lavender are all there; your eyes, on the same slider, and each eye a colour of its own if you like; your hairstyle, from seventeen: short, spiky, long, a ponytail, pigtails, a buzz cut, a bob, a curly crop, shoulder-length curls, an afro, a puff, locs, cornrows, long waves, space buns, a long braid or bald; your shirt; and pants, shorts or a skirt. There's no boy or girl to pick: anyone can wear anything.
  • Earned, and kept forever: one hat, one outfit and one accessory at a time, from what you've won. Nothing is for sale, and it carries over to every world and season.

The townsfolk are dressed the same way: each person in a town has a look of their own, the same on every visit, and a seaside town leans toward sun hats where a mountain one leans toward beanies. Nurses, clerks, guards and trainers keep their uniforms.

Private and retired worlds

When a season ends, your world becomes yours. You get a private copy of the world you played, with your team, your box and your progress, to keep playing at your own pace forever, with no daily limit and no season clock. A reset is a graduation, not a wipe. Meanwhile your server starts again on a brand-new map.

  • Worlds in the start menu switches between your server's world and every private world you have.
  • In a direct message, with no private world yet, you choose a region for a new one before your partner; in a server nothing changes.
  • Private worlds have the daily goal too, on the UTC day, but nothing you do in them counts on the leaderboards.
