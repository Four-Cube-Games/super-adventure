Super Adventure

Player Handbook

Mark is building a creature-collecting adventure, played inside Discord, with your friends, on a map nobody has seen before. This is every rule we've settled on so far. Some are as the classics did them; plenty are new. Tell us which ones you'd change.

How to weigh in

1. Read a ruleEach card says what happens and why.
2. Answer its questionThe yellow box is the thing we're least sure about.
3. Press "Give feedback"It opens a form on GitHub with the rule filled in, and your note lands on the feedback board. Hate a rule? Say so. Love one? Say that too.

Chapter 1

Getting started

On the alpha server you walk the world in a Discord Activity, opened with /activity: tile by tile, with a start menu for your party, bag and map. Other servers still play with slash commands and buttons until they're switched over. Either way, everything you see is private to you, so a whole server can play in one channel without spamming it.

/start in a server joins that server's shared world. /start in a direct message makes a world just for you. /play opens your game. /leaderboard and /worlds do what they say.

Your starter is one of three, but not always the famous three

Every world offers three starters: uncommon creatures that can evolve, each of a different type. One server might choose between a fox-like one, a dragon-like one and a leafy one. Everyone in a server is offered the same three.

Would you rather always get a grass, a fire and a water starter?

Give feedback

You set out with 5 balls and 500 coins

Enough to catch a few things before your first trainer battle pays out. The classics started you with 3,000 money and no balls at all.

Is that too stingy?

Chapter 2

The map

Every world is generated from a random seed, so each server gets a different region: different routes, different gym types, different creatures in different places. Each world is also one region, and it keeps that region's creatures and moves for good.

Places have made-up names of their own: towns like Barleymoor Town, a City wherever there's a gym, Basalt Cave, Thunder Bike Road, Lantern Shrine and hideouts like the Old Foundry. Routes are numbered in order. No two places in a world share a name.

Red squares are towns with a gym, marked gold once you've got the badge. Blue squares are towns without one. The wild: sand-colored routes, brown caves, gray tunnels, pale blue bike roads. Dark purple is a villain hideout, violet a shrine, and gold, at the very end, the Elite Four. Gray ground hasn't been opened yet. You stand in the red cursor.
Towns show their buildings: the Center (red roof), the shop (blue), a gym with a star that turns gold once you've got its badge, and the preserve's gate if the town has one. Routes, caves and hideouts are laid out fresh for every world, with tall grass, ledges, boulders and the trainers waiting there, faded once you've beaten them. At a shrine the legend sleeps on its altar as a shadow, until you catch it or beat it. If it gets away, it wakes again once you've earned enough worthiness.

A world is one region, with that game's dex and moves

There are nine regions, and a world is one of them: it has that region's creatures and they learn the moves they learned there. In the last three days of a season, everyone playing in your server votes on where the next one goes with /region vote, and can change their vote until it closes; the season you're in stays where it is. The most votes wins, a tie is drawn between the leaders, and with no votes at all it's the bot's home region. /region show has the tally. A private world can pick its own when you /start it in a direct message.

Which region would you vote for next season, and is three days long enough to vote?

Give feedback

Eight gyms, each a random type

Gym types are drawn from the region, as different from each other as possible. Your eighth gym might be Fairy. Levels climb steadily: the first gym leader is level 14, the last level 56. The way to the first gym starts gently, with at least three stretches of wild beginning at level 2 and climbing no more than three levels each.

Should gym types follow the originals (Rock, Water, Electric…) instead?

Give feedback

Gym trainers first, back to back

Every gym has trainers before its leader: two at the first gym, four by the eighth, using the gym's type a few levels below the leader. You fight them in order with no healing in between, and the leader won't see you until they're all down. Lose to anyone, or step out to heal at the Center, and they're all waiting again. They stand in the entrance hall and on the dais before the leader.

Too harsh, or just what a gym should feel like?

Give feedback

Every gym is a puzzle before its leader

Each gym is built around a puzzle, chosen by its leader's type. Ice gyms have floors you slide across. Electric ones have red and blue switches that raise and lower barriers. Psychic ones have warp panels. Rock, ground and fighting gyms have crates to push. Fire gyms have cracked floors that break after you cross; step into a hole and you're back at the start. Ghost and dark gyms are a dark maze, dark even with Flash. Flying and steel gyms have arrow tiles that carry you. Every other gym has aisles where the trainers stand. Later gyms have bigger puzzles. Once you hold a gym's badge, it comes back solved when you rerun it.

Are the puzzles a fun break from battles, or in the way?

Give feedback

Rare creatures hide in one spot each, and some aren't in your world at all

A few rare ones hide in a single place each, usually a cave. The rest simply don't exist in that world, so which ones you can't get changes from world to world. That's what makes playing more than one season worth it.

Is "some rares just aren't here" exciting, or annoying?

Give feedback

Where you are changes what you meet

Routes have lots of trainers and common creatures. Caves have few trainers and more unusual ones, on one to three floors: deeper caves have more, and the last cave before the Elite Four has three. Walk off the top edge to go up a floor and off the bottom to come back down. Each floor has its own trainers and item balls. Bike roads and tunnels are packed with trainers, and there's no way around them.

Anything you'd expect a cave or a route to have that we've missed?

Give feedback

Villain hideouts: three or four grunts, then a boss

From the third gym on, a couple of hideouts sit in the way. You fight straight through with nowhere to heal, and if you black out you start the hideout again from the first grunt. Bigger hideouts have up to three floors, the boss on the top one; early ones never have more than two. Every floor has a puzzle between its grunts and the way up: switches and barriers, warp panels, or arrows. Once you've walked past one it stays solved for good, and coming down onto a floor from above counts as having passed it. If you get stuck, "Stuck?" in the menu puts the puzzle you're in back to its start.

Is losing the whole run too harsh?

Give feedback

Every stretch has a look, and routes have hills and water

Each stretch of wild between towns is a meadow, forest, coast, mountain, snow, desert, volcano or swamp. The way to the first gym is all meadow; after that the land shifts gradually, and a gym's type pulls its stretch toward its home: a water gym sits by the coast, an ice gym in the snow, a fire gym near a volcano. The last stretch, to the Elite Four, is mountain. A biome also tilts the wild creatures on its routes towards its own kinds: water and flying on the coast; ground, rock and fire in the desert; ice and water in the snow; fire and rock by a volcano; grass and bug in the forest; poison, water and grass in the swamp; rock, fighting, flying and dragon in the mountains. Meadows are as before, and trainers' teams are unchanged. Routes are bigger now, with terraces joined by stairs (you can only climb them north or south, never a cliff), rivers with bridges, lakes, and the sea along the coast. Ground across a stream or the water is reached with Surf, once you've earned it. The art comes from tile packs by other artists, listed under Credits in Options.

Do the routes feel too big, or too winding, now?

Give feedback

Every town has a character

Towns are big now, and each is laid out by where it is: a port has a harbor, a mountain village sits on a terrace, and there are oases, farming villages, market plazas, woodland villages, mushroom hamlets and hot springs. The buildings are the size of real ones. Each town also has one special building to match: a farming barn gives you 3 healing berries, a market's department store sells everything the shops have reached so far, a woodland ranger's lodge gives 5 of the best ball, a port lighthouse gives an evolution item, an oasis seer's tent names the next shrine and today's roamers, a mountain dojo gives a held item that sometimes lets its holder move first once you beat its master, a mushroom herbalist gives 3 cures, and a hot spring bathhouse heals your team. Every gift is given once; the bathhouse heals as often as you like. Every town grows its own streets: a main road with bends, side streets that turn or end in little plazas, and a square with a well, fountain or pool. Buildings face the streets, yards have fences, gardens and trees, and lamps, benches and barrels line the way. A port grows along a quay with piers, a mountain village climbs terraces by stairs, a farming village keeps fenced fields, a market town lines the road with stalls, an oasis grows around a pool, and a hot-spring town has steaming pools in its yards. Cities are bigger the further you go: through the fourth gym a city is two parts, the town and then the gym district; the fifth and sixth gyms' cities add a market quarter, and the seventh and eighth a park with a canal too. You arrive in the town part, with the Center, shop, houses and the preserve's gate, and walking on through a far edge takes you to the next part, and the last part is always the gym district, a formal garden, a harbor, a market square or a mountain terrace by the town's character. The way on to the next stretch leaves from the gym district.

A mountain dojo fights: two Black Belts stand at the sides and a master at the top, and only beating the master earns the gift. Until then the doorkeeper tells you to beat the master first. Once a world, in the first town without a gym after the third gym, there may be a haunted tower (some worlds have none). It has three floors climbed by stairs, two mediums on each floor with ghost-type teams, and mist on the upper floors that wakes wild ghosts. A keeper at the top gives 3 revives, once. Tower and dojo trainers count as beaten like any trainer, and don't ask for rematches.

Which kind of town would you like to see more of?

Give feedback

Badges teach you to cut, smash, push and surf

Your badges open new ways across the land: Cut with the first, Flash the second, Rock Smash the third, Strength the fourth, Surf the fifth and Waterfall the seventh. They take no move slot, and you don't need any particular creature: badges are enough. Walk into a small tree, a cracked rock, a boulder or the water, and whoever in your party suits the move best does it, and the game says who. Grass, bug, steel, dark and normal creatures cut best; fighting, rock, ground and steel ones, or anything with a strong Attack, smash and push; water ones surf and climb waterfalls; electric, psychic, fire and fairy ones light up caves. If none of them fits, any healthy creature does it. Trees, rocks and boulders are back next time you come in. They only ever open side paths, never the way through, so nobody is stuck. While you surf, nothing wild bothers you, for now.

Is a fitting type the right way to choose who helps, or should creatures have to learn it?

Give feedback

Item balls are worth the detour

Balls lying on the ground hold an item. Look behind small trees and, from the fourth gym, cracked rocks and boulders, across streams if you can surf, and loose about routes and caves. You can find each one once, what's inside is the same for everyone in a world, and it gets better further along the map.

Do you like hunting for them, or would you rather items came from somewhere else?

Give feedback

Deep caves are dark until someone can light them

Caves in the second half of the map show only a circle around you. With a creature in your party that can use Flash, you see the whole cave. Every floor of Victory Road, the last cave before the Elite Four, has a crate puzzle, solved for good once you've walked past it.

Is a dark cave a fun puzzle or a chore?

Give feedback

Everyone has a name, and something to say

Trainers suit where they stand: a Youngster or a Lass on a route, Hikers underground, Bikers on the bike road, Ace Trainers in a gym, Grunts in a hideout. Each one has a line when the fight starts and another when it ends: “I've been lost for three days. Battle me so it feels like progress.” Leaders talk about their type and hand over the badge themselves. In every town a local has something to say that changes each day, and it's often a real tip about what's ahead, like where something rare hides or what type the next leader uses. The nurse, the shop clerk and the preserve's warden say hello too.

Which line made you smile, and which one should we rewrite?

Give feedback

Chapter 3

Days, and playing together

This is the biggest difference from the games. The map is split into stretches that each end at a town, and you can push into one new stretch a day, or more if your server picks a faster pace. Going back over ground you've already covered is always free.

One new stretch of map per day, unless your server speeds up

Leaving a town for ground you've never seen costs your day's move. Inside a stretch you can go as far as you like, so you can always finish a day somewhere safe. At a faster pace you get two or three new stretches a day instead.

Would you play more or less if this limit weren't there?

Give feedback

A new day starts at midnight where your server is

Each server picks its time zone with /timezone set, suggested as you type. Until it does, days turn over at midnight UTC. Changing it never costs or gives anyone a day: the day you're in just runs on to the next midnight in the new zone, so it can get longer, but never shorter. Private worlds stay on UTC.

Is midnight the right time for a new day, or would the morning suit your server better?

Give feedback

The first person to reach a town opens the way for everyone

A server moves through the map together. Nobody can go past the furthest town anyone has reached, and whoever gets there first holds the door for the rest. The server hears about it: "Ada reached Fernford City first."

Fun race, or frustrating wait?

Give feedback

Healing at a Center ends your day

Heal up and you're done until tomorrow. Or pay coins to keep going, which costs twice as much each time you do it in one day (200, then 400, then 800). You never have to heal to finish a day: it ends by itself at midnight, and once you've used today's new ground you can keep searching, battling and training where you are.

Is ending the day the right price for a full heal?

Give feedback

Blacking out costs half your coins, but not your day

You're patched up and sent back to the last Center you passed. If blacking out ended your day too, fainting on purpose would be a free heal and nobody would ever use a Center.

Half your coins: too much, too little?

Give feedback

Chapter 4

Battles and catching

Battles work like the classics: faster creatures go first, same-type moves hit harder, type matchups matter, moves can poison, paralyze or put to sleep, you can swap creatures mid-fight, and you can run from wild ones but not trainers. On top of that come later games' pieces: critical hits, abilities, weather and screens. Catching uses the original formula: the weaker it is, the easier it is to catch.

PSN poisoned and BRN burned: they lose an eighth of their HP every turn, and a burn halves physical attacks. PAR paralyzed: a quarter of the speed, and one turn in four it can't move. SLP asleep for one to three turns; FRZ frozen until it thaws, one turn in five.

Every throw uses a ball

Plain balls are the basic throw, Great Balls are 1.5 times as likely, Ultra Balls twice. A catch pays you 10 coins a level, so a good catch roughly pays for its ball.

Should catches pay coins at all?

Give feedback

One wild creature in 1,024 is shiny

A shiny comes in its own colors, with a sparkle beside it and a ✨ by its name, and a shiny encounter opens with “✨ A shiny Sparrowling appeared!” It's no stronger than any other. It stays shiny when you catch it and when it evolves, your server hears when you catch one, and your trainer card counts the shinies you own.

Is 1 in 1,024 rare enough to feel special, but common enough that you'll meet one?

Give feedback

You carry six, and the box holds everything else

Anything you catch with six already in your party goes to your box, and the box has no limit. A cap would only make you let creatures go to make room. The Party button swaps creatures between party and box whenever you're out of battle. They go as they are, so the box won't heal them.

Would you ever want to release a creature, or trade one away for good?

Give feedback

Everyone who fought shares the experience

Like the classics: every creature that was out against a foe and is still standing splits its experience evenly, and catching one gives the same experience as knocking it out. The third gym leader hands you the Exp. Share, and from then on the rest of your party takes half as well. It's part of that world's run, not something you keep: nothing you carry between worlds makes you better at the game.

Is the third badge the right time for the Exp. Share?

Give feedback

No two creatures are quite alike, and they grow by what they beat

Like the later games. Every wild creature, and your starter, has a nature: most raise one stat by a tenth and lower another, a few do neither. It also has potential, 0 to 31 in each stat, which your party screen grades S to D. Both are fixed for life, so a second Sparrowling can be a better Sparrowling. Beating a creature trains everyone who earns experience from it in the stat its species teaches: a small bird teaches Speed, a rock snake Defense. That's up to 252 in a stat and 510 in all. Trainers' creatures have more potential the higher up the trainer: the Elite Four have the most.

Is this fun to chase, or too much to keep track of?

Give feedback

Swap creatures whenever you like, for a turn

Call one back and send another out. It costs your turn, like using an item, and the other side gets a free hit on whoever comes in.

Is a free hit on the newcomer too harsh?

Give feedback

Gym leaders and champions switch back

Gym leaders, everyone in a villain hideout or guarding a shrine, the Elite Four and Hall of Fame champions read your creature's type. If it hits theirs hard, they call it back for one that takes it better, and when one falls they send out whichever suits the fight. Their ace always comes out last. They see your switch a turn late, and they never guess: the same position gets the same answer. Route and gym trainers send theirs out in order.

Should ordinary trainers switch too, or is that best kept for the big fights?

Give feedback

Beating wild creatures pays a little

Three coins a level. Trainers are the real money, at 25 coins a level of their strongest creature, so an early trainer pays about what a ball costs and a gym leader far more. Once you've beaten them all, though, there needs to be some way to afford balls again.

Is grinding wild creatures for coins something you'd do?

Give feedback

Poison, burns, paralysis, sleep and freeze last until they're cured

A creature has one at a time, and it carries it from battle to battle until a Center, a cure from the bag or a berry fixes it. Fire types can't be burned, electric types can't be paralyzed, and so on. Poison doesn't hurt between battles, because there's no walking. A sleeping or frozen creature is twice as easy to catch.

Should poison bite between battles, the way it did with every step in the classics?

Give feedback

Growl, Swords Dance and Confuse Ray are in

Creatures learn their status moves as well as their attacks. Stat changes go up to six steps either way and wear off when it's called back. Confusion wears off after a few turns and sometimes makes it hit itself. Attacks like Bite can make the target flinch.

Do opponents using Growl and Tail Whip slow battles down too much?

Give feedback

One hit in 24 is a critical hit

A critical hit does half as much again, and ignores the attacker's lowered Attack, the target's raised Defense and any screen. Slash, Razor Leaf and the like land them one time in eight. This is the later games' rule; in the classics fast creatures crit far more.

Do critical hits feel fair, or just random?

Give feedback

Recover, Rest, Giga Drain and Leech Seed are in

Recover and its like heal half your HP. Rest sleeps for two turns, cures whatever ailed it and wakes at full HP; hold a berry that cures sleep and it wakes at once. Draining moves heal half of what they deal, and Take Down and Double-Edge cost recoil. Leech Seed saps an eighth of the target's HP each turn for whoever is out on your side. Grass types can't be seeded. Dream Eater only works on a sleeping target. Stockpile stores up to three and toughens you as it does; Spit Up hits harder and Swallow heals more for each one stored, and both use them all up.

Does Recover make battles drag?

Give feedback

Screens and weather last five turns

Reflect halves physical damage to your side and Light Screen halves special, and they stay up if you swap. Rain powers up water moves and weakens fire ones, and harsh sun does the reverse. Sandstorm and hail chip everyone a sixteenth a turn, except rock, ground and steel types in sand and ice types in hail. Snow, from the newest games, hurts nobody but toughens ice types' Defense, and a sandstorm toughens rock types' Special Defense. The classics had no weather, so in a world of the first region only abilities bring it. You can see it, too: rain falls, sand blows and snow drifts across the battle picture.

Would you build a team around weather?

Give feedback

Quick Attack goes first

Some moves jump the queue whatever the speed: Quick Attack, Extreme Speed, Aqua Jet. A few, like Roar and Counter, always go last. Between two moves of the same kind, the faster creature goes first, as ever. Prankster puts its user's status moves ahead.

Do quick moves make slow creatures worth using?

Give feedback

Fury Attack, Wrap and Hyper Beam work like the later games

Moves that hit two to five times hit two or three times most often, and each hit can crit. Wrap, Bind and Fire Spin trap the target for four or five turns, taking an eighth a turn. A trapped creature can't run or swap, but it can still fight, unlike in the classics. Hyper Beam costs you the next turn to recharge.

Is trapping annoying or fun when it's done to you?

Give feedback

Every creature has an ability

Each has one of its species' usual abilities, and keeps it when it evolves. One wild creature in twenty has its species' hidden ability instead, and your party screen marks it. Levitate dodges ground moves, Intimidate lowers the other's Attack as it comes out, Swift Swim doubles speed in rain, Static may paralyze whoever hits it, and so on. When one does something, the battle says so. Your party screen says what each one does. Around two in three do something here. The rest are for exploring or double battles, which this game doesn't have. Abilities that care about the kind of move work as in the games: Static only triggers on moves that make contact, Iron Fist powers up punches, and Soundproof blocks sound moves. Opponents know your ability too: they won't throw an electric move at a Volt Absorb.

Is one in twenty the right rate for hidden abilities?

Give feedback

Teach a move back whenever you like

With four moves full, a new one gets skipped, but it isn't lost. From the bag, a creature can swap any move it knows for one it's learned by its level. The new move takes over whatever share of PP the old one had left, so swapping isn't a free heal.

Should relearning cost something, like the Move Reminder did?

Give feedback

Chapter 5

Evolution

Creatures evolve the way they did in their own games: by level, by stone, or, for the ones that needed a trade, by trading or a Linking Cord.

By level, after a battle you win

Once a creature has reached its level, it evolves when the battle's over, one step at a time. It learns anything its new form learns at that level. A fainted creature waits for the next win, and nothing evolves after a loss.

Is waiting until after the battle the right moment?

Give feedback

Hold an Everstone to stay as you are

Shops sell the Everstone for 3,000 coins. While a creature holds it, it won't evolve by leveling up, and it's never used up. Take it back and the creature evolves after its next win. A stone or the Linking Cord still works on it, because using one is already your choice. Now and then a wild creature is found holding one.

Would you rather have a "don't evolve" button after each win?

Give feedback

Stones, as in the games

Some creatures need a particular stone, and for others the stone you choose decides which form they take. Use one on a creature from your bag. Shops sell stones from the third gym's town, at 3,000 each.

Is 3,000 about right for a stone?

Give feedback

Trade evolutions happen when you trade, or with a Linking Cord

Trade Kadabra, Machoke, Graveler or Haunter to a friend and it evolves as it arrives, unless it's holding an Everstone. With nobody to trade with, a Linking Cord from the shop does the same, the way a later game did it. It costs 8,000.

Would you trade a creature away just to evolve it, knowing you'd have to ask for it back?

Give feedback

Every hideout's boss leaves a stone behind

Clear a villain hideout and its boss drops an evolution item: one that evolves something you've got, if anything you've got needs one. It leaves a piece of held gear too (see Shops and items).

What else should bosses and gym leaders give you?

Give feedback

Each creature holds one item

Chapter 7

The preserve

Our catching preserve. One town in the middle of the map, somewhere between the fourth and sixth gyms, has a fenced preserve behind it.

500coins to get in
20balls in the bag
2rares found nowhere else
0coins paid per catch

The preserve's rules

Your team never fights. Each turn you throw a ball, bait or a rock, or leave. Bait makes it stay but harder to catch; a rock makes it easier to catch but likelier to run. Fast creatures bolt often.

Are the preserve rules fun or frustrating?

Give feedback

Two of the rarest live only in there

Before the caves pick their rares, the preserve takes two. Usually it takes two of the region's famous rarities. If you want them in this world, the preserve is the only place.

Should the preserve have more, or different, creatures?

Chapter 8

Legendaries

Some of the region's legendaries sleep at shrines in the back half of the map. The rest roam, turning up about once in a hundred searches in the late wilderness, well above the level of anything around them.

Shrines are guarded by two to four "seekers" digging for the legend. Beat them all and it wakes. Roamers keep turning up, rarely, and aren't one-shot. Visitors: a world can borrow up to three legendaries from other regions installed on the bot. They only ever roam.

A legend that gets away can be earned back

Catch it or beat it and it's gone for good. If it runs or beats you, it watches you, and it'll face you again once you've proved yourself: 25 worthiness for a second try, 60 for a third, 120 each time after. Worthiness is what you do in the meantime: 1 per trainer beaten, 2 per new dex entry, 3 per hideout cleared, 5 per badge. Coins can't buy it. Your chances are yours alone, and a shrine's legend is six levels above that stretch's gym leader.

Too easy, too hard, or about right?

Give feedback

A wild legend fights half as hard again

Met at a shrine or roaming, a legend has half as much again in every stat except HP. The battle screen says so. Catch it and it loses that, and is only as strong as its level, so a caught legend doesn't break the rest of the game.

Should the aura be stronger, or cover HP too?

Give feedback

Beating a legend instead of catching it blesses your creature

Whichever creature lands the knockout is permanently 10% stronger in every stat. It's the one power-up training can't give you, so passing on the catch is a real choice.

Would you ever choose the blessing over the legend?

Give feedback

Chapter 9

Seasons

A server's world lasts a season. When it ends, the server starts again on a brand new map, and your character doesn't disappear.

Your server picks its pace

Server admins choose one of four with /pace set. The map is about fifteen stretches, so every pace leaves slack for real life.

Chapter 10

Winning, and after

Beat all eight gyms and then the Elite Four, fought back to back with nowhere to heal, and you've won the map.

The same Elite Four in every region

The Elite Four (Ice, Fighting, Ghost and Dragon) and the Champion (Normal) wait at the end of every world, whatever the region. Their teams change from world to world, but always come from that region, stick to their own type and are fully evolved and strong: no weak novelties, no legendaries. When a region is short of a type, they bring a lesser form or a second copy of something strong.

Your winning team enters the Hall of Fame

The team is saved exactly as it was when you won: every creature, level and move. Your server hears about it, a trophy goes on the back of your trainer card, and your team can turn up in other people's Elite Four rematches.

After you win, real champions take the Elite Four's seats

Once a day you can run the Elite Four again, and other players' Hall of Fame teams sit in it, from any region: a champion from one region brings their team into another region's world, with their exact creatures, levels and moves. Trade with anyone in your server

/trade offer @friend puts up one of yours, from your party or box. They answer with one of theirs using /trade answer, you accept, and they confirm, from /trade list. Until it's done or called off, both creatures wait on offer, out of your party. A traded creature arrives rested, but can't be traded on until its new owner heals at a Center, so nobody trades just to skip one. Your server hears about finished trades.

Challenge a friend's team

/challenge @friend in your server asks them in the channel. If they take it on, you fight a copy of their team, played by the bot the way champions play: switching to suit the fight and saving their best for last. Your team walks in fit too, so there's no need to heal first. No items, and running means conceding. The result goes on both your win-loss records, with a tally between you two: "You lead 3–1." There's no experience or money in it.

A raid boss every week, brought down together

A week into the season, and every week after, something strong turns up in your server's world for the day: fully evolved, a little above the gym you should have reached by now, and never a legendary. It has HP enough for everyone playing. /raid shows it, and you can fight it three times with a fit copy of your team, so it costs you nothing. Whatever you knock off counts for everyone. When it falls, everyone who helped gets one throw at catching it: likely, but not certain. Catch it, and it joins you at the boss's level.

Each server picks what gets announced

By default it's only the big moments: a new season, someone opening the way to a new town, a legendary caught, a new Hall of Fame entry, the vote on the next region opening, and the week's raid turning up and going down. Chapter 11

Your trainer

You're not a background character. Your trainer stands on every map, steps into every battle and has a trainer card, dressed how you choose, in things you've earned.

Dress how you like

Skin tone, hair color, hairstyle (short, spiky, long, a ponytail or pigtails), shirt, and pants, shorts or a skirt are free to change whenever you want. There's no boy or girl to pick: anyone can wear anything. On top of that you wear one hat, one outfit and one accessory, from what you've earned.

Earn the rest, and keep it forever

Nothing is for sale. A cap for your first badge, a hoodie for two, a backpack for three, a beanie for four and a champion's jacket for all eight. A bandana for clearing a hideout, glasses, a sun hat, a ranger vest and a lab coat as your dex fills, a scarf from a legend's blessing, a cape for the Hall of Fame, and a crown for beating a rematch full of champions. They carry over to every world and season.
